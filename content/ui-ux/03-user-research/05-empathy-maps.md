# Empathy maps

*An empathy map is a one-page snapshot of what someone says, thinks, does and feels, so you can spot the gaps between them.*

## TL;DR

An empathy map is a one-page snapshot of what someone says, thinks, does and feels, so you can spot the gaps between them.

---

## Chapter 1: The delayed train

You're on a station platform. The announcement says your train is 40 minutes late. To your friend you say, "Fine, no problem." In your head you're thinking, "I'm going to miss my interview." Your hands are refreshing the railway app every ten seconds, and you keep checking the time. Underneath it all, you feel anxious.

Four layers, one person. If you only listened to what they said, you'd miss almost everything. An empathy map puts those layers side by side.

**In real life:** The person who says "I'm fine" while refreshing the app for the fifth time. What they say and what they do don't match. That mismatch is what you're hunting for.

---

## Chapter 2: The four quadrants

- **Says:** What they tell you, in their own words. Direct quotes work best.
- **Thinks:** What's going on in their head. Often something you infer from how they talk.
- **Does:** What you can actually see them doing. Actions and behavior.
- **Feels:** The emotions underneath. Also usually inferred.

A useful honesty rule: Says and Does come from what you saw and heard. Thinks and Feels are often your best guess. Mark your guesses so you know they need checking.

---

## Chapter 3: Fill one in

1. **Pick one person.** Use someone you interviewed, or one persona.
2. **Draw four boxes.** Says, Thinks, Does, Feels.
3. **Fill Says and Does first.** Use real quotes and real actions from your notes.
4. **Fill Thinks and Feels next.** Add a question mark to anything you're guessing.
5. **Look for gaps.** Where does Says not match Does? Where is a strong feeling with no action at all? Gaps are where the opportunities are.

---

## Chapter 4: Where it fits

An empathy map works well right after interviews and before you write a persona or a problem statement. It's also a fast way to get a team looking at the same person.

Keep it to one page. And it doesn't replace research. It organizes what you already learned.

---

## Common mistakes

- Filling it from imagination
- Mixing up Thinks and Feels
- Making one map for "everyone"
- Never looking for the gaps

---

## Try it (15 min)

- [ ] Use your notes from one interview.
- [ ] Draw the four boxes and fill Says and Does from real things.
- [ ] Fill Thinks and Feels, marking guesses with a question mark.
- [ ] Circle one gap between what they said and what they did.
