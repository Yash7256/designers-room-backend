# Personas (without the cringe)

*A persona is a short, honest summary of a real pattern you found in research. If it didn't come from real people, it's fiction.*

## TL;DR

A persona is a short, honest summary of a real pattern you found in research. If it didn't come from real people, it's fiction.

---

## Chapter 1: The cringe persona

You've seen it. A smiling stock photo. "Priya, 24. Loves coffee, travel and yoga." A quote that sounds like an ad. It looks like design, and it tells you nothing about what to build.

The problem isn't personas. It's that most are invented, padded with details that have nothing to do with the product.

**In real life:** A persona that says "Rahul loves cricket and biryani" won't help you decide where a button goes. A persona that says "Rahul screenshots his notes because he can't find them again" will.

---

## Chapter 2: What a useful persona has

- **Context:** The situation they're in. Not just age and city.
- **Goal:** What they're trying to get done.
- **Behavior:** What they do today, including clumsy workarounds.
- **Frustrations:** What gets in their way.
- **A real quote:** Taken from your interviews, with permission and no names.

Keep it to one page. If it needs more, it's trying to cover too many people.

---

## Chapter 3: Build one from your interviews

1. **Group your notes.** Put similar behaviors and goals together.
2. **Find one to three clear patterns.** Each one becomes a possible persona.
3. **Write each as a short profile.** The name is just a label to make it easy to talk about.
4. **Add one real quote.** Anonymous is fine.
5. **Add a "so we should..." line.** What does this pattern mean for design? This is the line that makes a persona useful.

Be upfront about what it's based on. If it's built from four interviews, call it an early persona, and say so on the page.

---

## Chapter 4: Use it or lose it

A persona only matters if it shows up in decisions. Ask out loud: "Would Rahul understand this label?" or "Would this still work if he's in a hurry?"

Keep the number small, one to three is plenty. Update them as you learn. And remember a persona is a summary, not a real person, so keep talking to real ones whenever you can.

---

## Common mistakes

- Inventing personas without research
- Filling them with hobbies and demographics
- Making ten personas
- Writing one and never using it
