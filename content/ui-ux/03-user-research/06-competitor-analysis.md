# Competitor analysis

*Look at what people use today, learn what works and what doesn't, and find the gap. Learn from others, don't copy them.*

## TL;DR

Look at what people use today, learn what works and what doesn't, and find the gap. Learn from others, don't copy them.

---

## Chapter 1: Three chai stalls

There are three chai stalls near your college. One is the cheapest. One is the fastest. One makes the best chai but always has a queue. You pick based on what you need that day.

That's competitor analysis. Each option wins on something different, and understanding why helps you see where a gap might be.

---

## Chapter 2: Who counts as a competitor?

Don't limit yourself to products that look like yours.

- **Direct:** Same solution for the same people. Another study-planner app, if you're building one.
- **Indirect:** A different solution to the same problem. A WhatsApp group where friends share timetables.
- **Workarounds:** Pen and paper, a screenshot folder, or just doing nothing and hoping.
- **Inspiration:** Products from a totally different field that solve a similar kind of problem well.

Often your fiercest competitor is the habit people already have.

---

## Chapter 3: Compare with a simple table

1. **Choose three to five options.** A mix of direct, indirect and workarounds.
2. **Choose four to six criteria that matter to your user.** Not a long list of features. Think "time to finish the task", "how clear it is", "how much it costs", "how much you trust it".
3. **Try each one yourself.** Do the same task on each and note where you hesitate, like the teardown from Stage 01.
4. **Read reviews carefully.** Look for themes, not for the final verdict.
5. **Find the gap.** Where does everyone struggle? What does no one do well?

## Chapter 4: Learn without copying

Study why something works for its users, not just what it looks like. A layout that suits someone else's users may not suit yours.

Stay on solid ground: use public information and your own testing. Don't state facts about a company unless you've checked them. And don't claim you're "better" without evidence.

**In real life:** App store reviews. They're useful for finding themes, like "it keeps logging me out". But people with strong feelings are the ones who write them, so don't treat them as the whole picture.

---

## Common mistakes

- Making a long feature checklist instead of comparing what matters to users
- Only looking at direct competitors
- Copying screens instead of learning from the thinking
- Stating facts about companies without checking them
