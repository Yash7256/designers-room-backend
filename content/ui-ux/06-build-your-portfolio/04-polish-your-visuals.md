# Polish your visuals

*Good visuals make your thinking easier to see. Use clear hierarchy, consistent spacing and a few strong images instead of every screen.*

## TL;DR

Good visuals make your thinking easier to see. Use clear hierarchy, consistent spacing and a few strong images instead of every screen.

---

## Chapter 1: Plating, not cooking

Cooking is the real work, and in your case that's the research, the decisions and the design. Plating is how it looks when it arrives. Good plating doesn't make a bad dish good, but bad plating can hide a great one.

Polishing your visuals is plating. The goal is to make your thinking easy to notice.

**In real life:** A great meal served in a messy heap on a chipped plate. The taste is there, but nobody's in the mood. Your case study works the same way.

---

## Chapter 2: Show the best, cut the rest

You don't need every screen. Pick around six to ten images that tell the story.

- **Hero image:** The strongest picture of your final design, near the top.
- **Process images:** Clean photos or screenshots of sketches, flows and wireframes that show your thinking.
- **Key screens:** The screens that carry the important decisions.
- **Results and learning:** Test findings, before and after comparisons, or a quote from a real user.

---

## Chapter 3: Make it look consistent

1. **Use your own system.** Same fonts, same spacing scale and same colors across the whole case study.
2. **Keep image sizes and margins consistent.** Messy edges quietly look careless.
3. **Show things in order.** A flow should read left to right or top to bottom.
4. **Annotate.** A small note pointing to the key part of a screen saves a paragraph of explanation.
5. **Apply the squint test.** Zoom out on your case study. What stands out first? Is it the right thing?
6. **Check contrast and text size.** Everything you learned about accessibility applies to your own portfolio.

---

## Chapter 4: Mockups and honest visuals

Device mockups are fine, and they can make screens easier to picture. But be careful with them.

- **Don't fake a launch.** A screenshot placed in a fake app store listing suggests it shipped.
- **Label concepts clearly.** Write "Concept" if it hasn't been built.
- **Don't pass stock photos off as user research.** Use real quotes and real photos only with permission.
- **Credit what you didn't make.** Photos, illustrations and icons from others need credit, and you must follow their license.
- **Export well.** Sharp images, but small enough to load fast. Check how it looks on a phone.

---

## Common mistakes

- Showing too many screens
- Screenshots too small to read
- Mixing different styles
- Using fake launch imagery for a project that isn't real

---

## Try it (30 min)

- [ ] Choose eight images for your case study.
- [ ] Arrange them in story order in a Figma frame.
- [ ] Do the squint test and note what stands out.
- [ ] Fix your three biggest issues, such as inconsistent margins, tiny text or low contrast.
