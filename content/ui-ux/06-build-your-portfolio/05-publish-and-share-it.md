# Publish & share it

*A link beats a PDF in a folder. Publish it somewhere people can open in one click, share it, and ask for real feedback.*

## TL;DR

A link beats a PDF in a folder. Publish it somewhere people can open in one click, share it, and ask for real feedback.

---

## Chapter 1: Put the dish on the table

You've cooked something you're proud of. Now it's sitting in the kitchen. Nobody can taste it.

A case study in a folder on your laptop helps no one. Publishing makes it real. It also makes it feel scary, and that's normal. Do it anyway.

**In real life:** A case study sitting in your Drive can't get you feedback, a message or an opportunity. A link can.

---

## Chapter 2: Where to publish

There's no single right place. These are common options, and each has trade-offs. Features change, so check each site's current details.

- **A portfolio platform (like Behance):** Quick to set up and built for creative work. You work within its layout.
- **Your own website:** Full control and a link that's truly yours. Takes more time to build and maintain.
- **A document page (like a Notion page):** Fast and simple. Fine to start with, if it's neat and readable.
- **A PDF:** Handy as an attachment alongside a link. It shouldn't be your only version.

You can start with one and add others later.

---

## Chapter 3: Before you hit publish

- [ ] The title says what the project is.
- [ ] The first screen has a summary: what it is, your role, and the outcome.
- [ ] Every link works.
- [ ] Images load properly and look good on a phone.
- [ ] Text is readable on a small screen.
- [ ] No private details or names without permission.
- [ ] Nothing confidential, and any agreements you've made are respected.
- [ ] Credits are in place for anything you didn't make.
- [ ] You've checked for typos.
- [ ] One friend has read it start to finish.
- [ ] You're sharing the published link, not an edit link.

---

## Chapter 4: Share it and ask for feedback

1. **Write a short, human note.** What it is, what you learned, and one question.
2. **Share it with real people.** Friends, classmates, communities, your social profiles.
3. **Ask specific questions.** "Was the problem clear on the first screen?" gets a better answer than "thoughts?".
4. **Collect feedback.** Note repeated points. One comment is a hint. Several are a signal.
5. **Update and keep going.** Improve it, and keep a short list of what you changed.
6. **Add it to your resume and profiles.** One line about the project plus the link.

We can't promise any particular result. Publishing, asking for feedback and improving is how you get better at this. Once the Designer's Room community and Weekly Challenge open, you'll be able to share your work there too.

---

## Common mistakes

- Sharing only a PDF
- Posting a link with no context
- Waiting for it to be perfect
- Ignoring feedback

---

## Try it (20 min)

- [ ] Publish your case study on one platform.
- [ ] Open it on your phone and fix anything that looks off.
- [ ] Send it to three people with one specific question.
- [ ] Write down the feedback you get.
