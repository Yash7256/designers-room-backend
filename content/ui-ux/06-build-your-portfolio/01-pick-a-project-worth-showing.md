# Pick a project worth showing

*One real project, finished well, beats five rushed ones. Pick the one that shows how you think.*

## TL;DR

One real project, finished well, beats five rushed ones. Pick the one that shows how you think.

---

## Chapter 1: The dish you serve to guests

When guests come over, you don't serve everything in the fridge. You pick one dish you can make well and tell the story behind it: where the recipe came from, what went wrong the first time, what you changed.

A portfolio works the same way. It isn't a gallery of everything you've ever made. It's evidence that you can think, make decisions and finish things.

**In real life:** Twelve half-finished screens that don't connect, versus one project with a clear problem, a messy middle and an honest ending. People remember the second one.

---

## Chapter 2: What makes a project worth showing

- **A real problem:** A problem someone actually has. Redesigns are fine, if they start from real research.
- **A clear role:** What you did, and what someone else did.
- **A process you can explain:** Research, flow, wireframes, testing, or at least the parts you did.
- **Something visible at the end:** A finished design, a tested prototype, or a clear lesson.
- **Something you're proud of:** You'll be talking about it, so pick one you enjoy explaining.

---

## Chapter 3: Where to find one

You probably have one already. Look back at the checkpoints you did.

1. **Combine your checkpoints.** Stage 03 gave you research, an early persona and a problem statement. Stage 04 gave you a flow, wireframes and testing. Stage 05 gave you a component library. Together, that's a full project story.
2. **Solve a real problem nearby.** A college club, a family shop, a hostel problem. Ask permission first.
3. **Redesign something, with research.** Pick a product people complain about, and do your own interviews and testing before you redesign.
4. **Volunteer.** A local group or student club often needs help and will give you a real problem.

---

## Chapter 4: Be honest about what it is

Say what the project actually is: a class project, a solo concept, a self-initiated redesign, or real work for someone. Don't pretend it shipped if it didn't. Don't claim a team's work as yours.

People reading your portfolio will ask follow-up questions, so what you say needs to hold up. Honest and clear reads as confidence.

---

## Common mistakes

- Showing everything you've ever made
- Picking a project with no research behind it
- Claiming team work as solo work
- Choosing the prettiest project instead of the one you can explain best

---

## Try it (15 min)

- [ ] List three projects you could show.
- [ ] Score each from 0 to 2 on: real problem, clear role, explainable process, visible result, proud of it.
- [ ] Pick the winner.
- [ ] Write one honest sentence about what it is, like "A self-initiated concept based on five interviews with hostel students."
