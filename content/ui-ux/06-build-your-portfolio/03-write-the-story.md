# Write the story

*Write like you're explaining it to a smart friend over chai. Short, specific and honest, with a point in every paragraph.*

## TL;DR

Write like you're explaining it to a smart friend over chai. Short, specific and honest, with a point in every paragraph.

---

## Chapter 1: The smart friend test

Imagine explaining your project to a friend who's clever but wasn't there. You wouldn't use jargon. You'd say what happened, in plain words.

| Jargon | Smart friend |
| --- | --- |
| I conducted user-centric research leveraging qualitative methods to uncover pain points. | I talked to five hostel students and noticed most of them screenshot their notes because they can't find them later. |

---

## Chapter 2: Specific beats impressive

1. **Start with the problem.** Not with your process.
2. **Be concrete.** Real details and real quotes beat vague claims.
3. **Use "I" for what you did.** And "we" only when it was really a team.
4. **Share what surprised you.** Surprises show you were paying attention.
5. **Admit what went wrong.** "What I'd do differently" reads as maturity, not weakness.
6. **One point per paragraph.** If a paragraph has two ideas, split it.

Use numbers only if they're real. "Five of six testers" is great if it's true. An invented number is worse than no number.

---

## Chapter 3: Headings and captions that work

Most people skim, so your headings should carry the point on their own.

- **Weak heading:** "Research"
- **Strong heading:** "Students screenshot their notes because they can't find them"

Give every image a caption that says what to notice. And add alt text for images, as you learned on the Accessibility page.

---

## Chapter 4: Edit ruthlessly

1. **Read it aloud.** Anything that makes you stumble should be rewritten.
2. **Cut your first paragraph.** It's often warm-up.
3. **Remove filler.** Words like "very", "really" and "basically" can usually go.
4. **Check every fact and number.** Every claim should be true and every number real.
5. **Ask a friend two questions.** "What is this project about?" and "What did I do?" If they can't answer in a sentence, rewrite.

Honesty check before you move on:

- [ ] Is every claim true?
- [ ] Is every number real?
- [ ] Have I credited anyone who helped?
- [ ] Have I said what was a concept and what was real?

---

## Common mistakes

- Walls of jargon
- Claiming results you can't back up
- Describing the process but never the insight
- Writing it in one sitting and never editing

---

## Try it (30 min)

- [ ] Write the Problem section in about 150 words.
- [ ] Rewrite any sentence that uses jargon.
- [ ] Replace one generic heading with a heading that states the takeaway.
- [ ] Ask a friend the two questions above.
