# Prototyping basics

*A prototype links your screens so people can click through them. It's for testing the flow, not for showing off animation.*

## TL;DR

A prototype links your screens so people can click through them. It's for testing the flow, not for showing off animation.

---

## Chapter 1: The flipbook

A flipbook is just a stack of still drawings. Flip through them fast and it feels like motion.

A prototype works the same way. Your screens are still pictures, and the links between them make it feel like a real app. It isn't one, but it's close enough for someone to try it.

**In real life:** Handing a friend your phone with a clickable prototype and asking, "Try booking a cab." You'll learn more from watching than from any amount of explaining.

---

## Chapter 2: Triggers, actions and animations

Every link in a prototype has three parts.

- **Trigger:** What the person does, like a click or tap, a drag, or hovering over something.
- **Action:** What happens, like going to another screen, opening an overlay, or scrolling.
- **Animation:** How the change looks, like an instant switch, a fade, a slide or Smart animate.
- **Starting point:** Where the flow begins when you press play.

Option names in Figma change over time, so look at what the current menu offers.

---

## Chapter 3: Wire up a flow

1. **Open the Prototype tab** in the right panel.
2. **Select a button.** Drag its connector to the frame it should lead to.
3. **Pick the trigger and action.** Usually On click and Navigate to.
4. **Add a starting point** to your first frame.
5. **Press Present** to play it like a real app.
6. **Share the prototype link** with people who'll test it. Check the sharing permissions first.

A tip: Smart animate matches layers that share the same name across frames, then animates the differences. So consistent layer names make smooth transitions possible.

---

## Chapter 4: Keep it purposeful

1. **Prototype only what you need to test.** One flow, not the whole app.
2. **Use overlays** for things like menus and modals instead of making whole new screens.
3. **Use animation to explain, not to decorate.** A slide can show where something came from. A bounce usually shows only that you found the bounce button.
4. **Match the detail to your question.** Testing the flow? Grey wireframes are fine. Testing how something feels? Add more detail.
5. **Remember its limits.** A prototype isn't the real app. Real speed, real data and real devices can feel different.

---

## Common mistakes

- Animating everything
- Prototyping every screen before testing anything
- Creating dead ends where testers get stuck
- Forgetting to set a starting point
