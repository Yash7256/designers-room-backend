# Typography basics

*Most of an interface is words. Make them easy to read.*

## TL;DR

Pick one or two typefaces, use a handful of sizes, and make text easy to read. Everything else is decoration.

---

## Chapter 1: Most of the interface is words

Open any app and count the pictures versus the words. Menus, buttons, prices, error messages, instructions: it's mostly text. So typography isn't a finishing touch. It's most of the design.

Here's the idea to hold onto: type's first job is to be read. Making it pretty is the second job.

**In real life:** A wedding card and a bus ticket use the same alphabet, but one sets a mood and the other does a job. Neither is wrong. Know which job your screen has.

---

## Chapter 2: Words designers use

- **Typeface:** The family of letter designs, like Inter or Georgia. People often say "font" for this too, and that's fine in everyday talk.
- **Weight:** How thick the strokes are, from thin to bold.
- **Line height:** The space from one line of text to the next. Too tight and lines crash into each other.
- **Serif vs sans-serif:** Serif letters have little strokes on their ends, like Times New Roman. Sans-serif letters don't, like Arial. Both can be very readable.

---

## Chapter 3: Make it easy to read

Four quick checks. These are common guidelines, not laws, so use your eyes too.

1. **Size.** Body text on screens is commonly 16 pixels or larger. Tiny text quietly shuts people out.
2. **Line height.** Roomy lines help eyes find the next line. For body text, a line height of about 1.4 to 1.6 times the size is a common starting point.
3. **Line length.** Very long lines tire readers. A commonly cited comfortable range is roughly 45 to 75 characters per line.
4. **Contrast.** Enough difference between text and background. We cover this properly on the Color & contrast page.

---

## Chapter 4: A tiny type scale

Instead of picking a new size every time, choose a small set and reuse it. A simple example looks like this (these numbers are just an example, not the answer).

1. **Title:** 32, bold. One per screen.
2. **Heading:** 24, medium. Starts a section.
3. **Subheading:** 18, medium. Starts a group.
4. **Body:** 16, regular. Most of your text.
5. **Caption:** 12 to 14, regular. Small notes and labels.

Use size and weight to separate levels, not ten different typefaces. One typeface in several weights is enough for most screens. If you add a second, keep it to a clear job, like headings only.

**In real life:** A good shop signboard has three sizes. The shop name is huge, "Since 1990" is small, and the phone number is readable from the road. Three sizes, one clear order.

---

## Common mistakes

- Using five different typefaces
- Light grey tiny text that's hard to read
- ALL CAPS paragraphs
- Center-aligning long paragraphs
