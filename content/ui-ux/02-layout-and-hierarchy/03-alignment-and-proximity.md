# Alignment & proximity

*Put related things close together and line things up. That's most of the fix.*

## TL;DR

Put related things close together and line things up. Do just those two and most "messy" screens fix themselves.

---

## Chapter 1: Same stuff, very different cupboard

Two cupboards hold exactly the same clothes. In one, everything is folded by type: shirts here, jeans there. In the other, everything is thrown in. The contents are identical, but only one of them is usable at 8 a.m. when you're late.

Screens work exactly the same way. Often you don't need to change what's on the screen at all. You just need to arrange it.

---

## Chapter 2: Proximity: close means "these belong together"

A group of psychologists in early 20th-century Germany, known as the Gestalt school, studied how people group what they see. One of their findings, usually called proximity, is simple: things that are close together look like they belong together.

This is why a label sits right above its own input field, closer to it than to the field above. If the gaps are equal, people have to guess.

**In real life:** A contacts list where each name sits right next to its own number. Space everything out evenly and you'd have to guess which number belongs to whom.

---

## Chapter 3: Alignment: invisible lines

When things share an edge, the eye sees order, even if it can't say why. When edges wobble by a few pixels, the screen feels slightly "off".

1. **Pick an edge.** Decide which line things will share, usually the left edge.
2. **Align with tools, not eyes.** Use Figma's alignment and snapping tools instead of nudging by feel.
3. **Keep paragraphs aligned to the start of the line.** In languages written left to right, that's left-aligned, which is easiest to read. In right-to-left languages, align to the right. Centered text is fine for short bits like a title.

---

## Chapter 4: Group with space first, lines and boxes second

Beginners often fix clutter by adding borders around everything. That adds more things to look at. Try space first.

- **Boxes everywhere:** Every item gets a border or card. The screen turns into a grid of rectangles and nothing stands out.
- **Space as grouping:** Small gaps inside a group, bigger gaps between groups. Fewer lines, calmer screen.

A form is a good example. Each label and field sits close together, with a larger gap before the next pair. No boxes needed.

---

## Common mistakes

- Even spacing between everything
- Center-aligning everything
- Borders around every item
- Aligning by eye

- [ ] In Figma, make a frame with six items: three labels and three input fields, all spaced evenly.
- [ ] Move each label close to its own field, and add a bigger gap between each pair.
- [ ] Align everything to the same left edge using the alignment tools.
- [ ] Compare it with your first version.
