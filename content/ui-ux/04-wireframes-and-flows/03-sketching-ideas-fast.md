# Sketching ideas fast

*Your first idea is rarely your best. Sketch lots of rough ideas quickly, then pick the best one.*

## TL;DR

Your first idea is rarely your best. Sketch lots of rough ideas quickly, then pick the best one.

---

## Chapter 1: Eight ways to make chai

Ask someone for new ways to make chai. The first idea is regular chai with a tweak. The second is the same. By the fifth or sixth, they say something odd and interesting that nobody had thought of.

That's how ideas work. The obvious ones come out first. You have to get through them to reach the good ones.

**In real life:** The first answer in any group brainstorm is usually the most obvious one. The interesting ideas show up after people run out of obvious ones. So keep going.

---

## Chapter 2: Why paper beats Figma right now

Paper is cheap, fast and takes no commitment. You can't polish a pencil sketch, and that's the point. When something costs ten seconds, you're happy to throw it away.

Open Figma too early and you start moving pixels, picking colors and getting attached. Save the screen for later.

You don't need to draw well. Boxes, stick figures and arrows are fine. The only job of a sketch is to show an idea.

---

## Chapter 3: The eight-in-eight drill

This exercise is often called Crazy 8s. It's simple.

1. **Fold a sheet of paper into eight boxes.**
2. **Set a timer for eight minutes.** About a minute per idea.
3. **Take a prompt.** Use one of your "How might we" questions from Stage 03.
4. **Fill one box per idea.** Don't erase and don't judge.
5. **Give each a short title.** Just a few words.

Then pick two or three ideas and spend a few more minutes turning each into a quick three-screen storyboard.

---

## Chapter 4: Choose without ego

1. **Lay your sketches out.** On a table or a wall, where you can see them all.
2. **Compare against the problem.** Which ones actually help your user? Not which one's prettiest.
3. **Mark the best parts.** An idea can borrow a piece from another idea.
4. **Keep one to three.** These are what you'll wireframe next.
5. **Let go of the rest.** You've lost nothing. They were ten-second sketches.

Working alone? Sleep on it, then look again. Or show a friend and ask, "What do you think this does?" Their answer tells you whether it's clear.

---

## Common mistakes

- Stopping after one idea
- Erasing and perfecting
- Skipping it because "I can't draw"
- Picking the prettiest sketch instead of the one that solves the problem

---
