# Low-fi wireframes

*A low-fi wireframe is a plain, grey-box blueprint of a screen. It's for deciding what goes where, not how it looks.*

## TL;DR

A low-fi wireframe is a plain, grey-box blueprint of a screen. It's for deciding what goes where, not how it looks.

---

## Chapter 1: Floor plan before furniture

When someone designs a house, they draw the floor plan first. Where does the kitchen go? How do you get from the door to the bedroom? Nobody picks sofa colors before deciding which room the sofa goes in.

A wireframe is the floor plan of a screen. If you jump to colors and photos too early, you end up arguing about the sofa while the kitchen is in the wrong room.

**In real life:** When people see a polished design, their feedback tends to drift toward colors and fonts. When they see plain grey boxes, the conversation stays on what matters: is this the right stuff in the right place?

---

## Chapter 2: What goes in, what stays out

- **In:** Layout and hierarchy. What goes where, and what's the biggest or most important thing.
- **In:** Real labels and key content. Use real words for buttons and headings wherever you can. "Lorem ipsum" hides problems.
- **Out:** Color and style. You'll add it later. Grey keeps everyone focused on structure.
- **Out:** Final images and polish. A grey box with a cross is enough to mean "photo here".

---

## Chapter 3: Build one in Figma

1. **Start from your flow.** One frame per step. Name them in order: "01 Home", "02 Search", "03 Results".
2. **Pick a real size.** A phone frame about 390 wide, or a desktop frame about 1440 wide, as in Stage 01.
3. **Use plain shapes.** Grey rectangles for images, simple text, plain buttons.
4. **Keep your habits.** Your spacing scale and three text styles from Stage 02 still apply. Hierarchy matters even in grey.
5. **Use real labels.** "Find a washing machine" beats "Button".
6. **Annotate.** Add small notes for anything that isn't obvious, like "opens a map when tapped".

---

## Chapter 4: Check it before you polish

Before adding any style, run these checks.

- [ ] Can you point to the main action on each screen?
- [ ] Does the squint test still work in grey?
- [ ] Could a stranger tell what each screen is for?
- [ ] Does every step in your flow have a screen?
- [ ] Have you shown empty, loading and error states, not just the perfect-data version?

If yes to all five, you're ready to test it. Don't polish yet. Polish comes after you know the structure works.

---

## Common mistakes

- Adding color and photos too soon
- Lorem ipsum everywhere
- Only designing the screen where everything goes right
- Skipping annotations

---

## Try it (30 min)

- [ ] Take three steps from your flow and build a grey wireframe for each.
- [ ] Use real labels and your three text styles.
- [ ] Name every frame in flow order.
- [ ] Run the squint test on each screen.
