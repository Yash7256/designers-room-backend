# User flows

*A user flow is a map of the steps someone takes to get something done. Draw it before you draw any screens.*

## TL;DR

A user flow is a map of the steps someone takes to get something done. Draw it before you draw any screens.

---

## Chapter 1: Screens are the last thing to design

Think about ordering at a busy chai stall. You reach the counter, say what you want, pay, wait, and collect. Nobody designed a "screen" here, but someone designed the flow: where you stand, when you pay, where the cups come out.

Apps work exactly the same way. If the flow is confusing, beautiful screens won't save it. That's why designers map the steps first.

**In real life:** Booking a cab. Open the app, enter a destination, choose a ride, confirm, wait, ride, pay, rate. That's a flow. Each arrow is a decision someone made about what happens next.

---

## Chapter 2: The basic pieces

- **Start and end:** Where the flow begins and where it finishes. Usually drawn as rounded shapes.
- **Step or screen:** One thing the person sees or does. Usually a rectangle.
- **Decision:** A question with different outcomes, like "Logged in?" Usually a diamond.
- **Arrow:** Which way the flow goes.

These shapes are common conventions from flowcharts. Clarity matters more than getting every shape perfect.

---

## Chapter 3: Draw one

1. **Pick one goal.** Take it from your problem statement, like "find out if a washing machine is free".
2. **Mark the start and finish.** Where is the person when they begin? What does "done" look like?
3. **List each step as a verb.** "Open the app", "choose a hostel", "see free machines". Verbs keep it about action.
4. **Add the decisions.** Where does the flow split? "Is the user signed in?"
5. **Add what goes wrong.** No internet, wrong input, the person changes their mind.

---

## Chapter 4: The happy path and everything else

The happy path is the flow where everything goes right. It's the easiest to draw and the least likely to be where the trouble is.

Most problems hide in the rest: alternate paths (other ways to reach the same goal), error paths (when something fails), and edge cases (a brand-new user, no data yet, a slow connection). Draw the happy path first, then walk through the rest.

Keep each flow to one goal. If a flow gets huge, split it into smaller ones. Paper, a whiteboard or FigJam all work fine.

---

## Common mistakes

- Starting with screens instead of steps
- Drawing only the happy path
- Squeezing everything into one giant flow
- Skipping what happens when something goes wrong

---

## Try it (25 min)

- [ ] Pick one goal, like booking a cab or using your own problem statement from Stage 03.
- [ ] Draw a flow of eight to twelve steps.
- [ ] Add at least one decision and one error path.
- [ ] Ask a friend to read it and tell you where they'd get lost.
