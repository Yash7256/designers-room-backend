# Testing early

*Test your rough wireframes with a few real people before you build anything. Watching a handful of people struggle teaches more than guessing for weeks.*

## TL;DR

Test your rough wireframes with a few real people before you build anything. Watching a handful of people struggle teaches more than guessing for weeks.

---

## Chapter 1: Hand someone your phone

You've made something and you're sure it's obvious. You hand it to a friend and say, "Try booking a cab." They tap the wrong thing. They squint. They say, "What does this do?"

In thirty seconds, you've learned more than you did in hours of staring at it yourself. That's the whole idea of usability testing.

**In real life:** The first time someone else uses something you made is always a bit humbling. That's not a bad thing. It's the best information you'll get all week.

---

## Chapter 2: Why test something rough?

- **It's cheap to fix.** Moving a box costs seconds. Changing built code costs days.
- **People are blunt about unfinished things.** There's no polished work to feel awkward about criticizing.
- **It stops you building the wrong thing.** Problems you find now never reach the code.

---

## Chapter 3: Run a simple test

1. **Pick three tasks.** Tied to your flow, like "Find and book a ride to the station".
2. **Make it clickable.** Link your frames in a Figma prototype, or use paper screens.
3. **Recruit about five people from your target group.** A small group of around five is a common starting point. It's enough to show the biggest problems, not every problem. Ask permission, and ask a parent or guardian first for anyone under 18.
4. **Set the tone.** Say, "We're testing the design, not you." Ask them to think aloud.
5. **Give a task, not instructions.** "Book a ride to the station", not "Tap the green button".
6. **Stay quiet and watch.** Don't help, explain or defend. Note where they hesitate, misclick or say "hmm".
7. **Debrief.** Ask, "What was confusing? What did you expect to happen?"

---

## Chapter 4: Turn it into fixes

1. **Write each issue as an observation.** "Three of five tapped Profile to find their orders." Not "people are dumb".
2. **Group similar issues.**
3. **Rank them by how much they block the goal.** Blocks the task is worst. Slows people down is next. Mildly annoying is last.
4. **Fix the biggest ones.** Then test again.

Look for repeats. One person's opinion is a hint. The same trouble spot for several people is a real problem. And don't defend your design in the room. Write it down and think later.

---

## Common mistakes

- Explaining the design while people test it
- Leading people ("Tap the green button")
- Testing with people who aren't your users
- Trying to fix every single comment

---

## Try it (25 min)

- [ ] Link your wireframes into a clickable Figma prototype.
- [ ] Test it with two friends on two tasks.
- [ ] Stay quiet and write down every hesitation.
- [ ] List your top three issues and one change you'll make.
